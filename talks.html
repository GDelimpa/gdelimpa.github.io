TU Delft, DCSC Colloquium · 2026

An Information Theory of Finite Abstractions: fundamental scalability limits and what to do next

How large must a finite abstraction be to represent a dynamical system accurately? I connect this question to rate–distortion theory, viewing abstractions as encoders of system trajectories to derive fundamental bounds on their size–accuracy tradeoff. The talk also illustrates how this perspective enables the construction of a minimal abstraction for a chaotic system.

IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC) · 2021

Abstracting the Sampling Behaviour of Stochastic Linear Periodic Event-Triggered Control Systems

Event-triggered control (ETC) reduces unnecessary sampling, but its sampling patterns become difficult to characterize in the presence of stochastic disturbances. I present an approach using interval Markov chains to bound the expected values of sampling-performance metrics expressed as discounted cumulative rewards, providing a formal way to assess stochastic ETC's sampling behaviour.

IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC) · 2020

Traffic Abstractions of Nonlinear Homogeneous Event-Triggered Control Systems

Scheduling communication in networks of event-triggered controllers requires models of when each controller may transmit. I present finite-state abstractions that capture the sampling behaviour of nonlinear homogeneous systems, extending an approach previously developed for linear systems. The talk also discusses how the construction could be extended to more general nonlinear dynamics.
