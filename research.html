01 / INFORMATION THEORY & FORMAL METHODS

The information theory of finite abstractions

Quantifying how small a finite model can be while retaining a prescribed level of accuracy.

Finite abstractions replace continuous dynamics with discrete models that retain all system behaviours, enabling formal verification. But accurate models can become prohibitively large. With Gabriel Gleizer, I connect this scalability problem to rate–distortion theory, the information theory of lossy compression: an abstraction encodes system trajectories, its size determines the rate, and its average deviation from the system determines the distortion.

For deterministic autonomous systems, we derive fundamental lower bounds on the size–accuracy tradeoff. These bounds reflect both trajectory entropy and the geometry of the trajectory set. For the chaotic doubling map, we also construct an abstraction that is optimal for the studied size–accuracy criterion.

Why it matters

This gives abstraction design a quantitative benchmark: it helps distinguish limitations imposed by the dynamics from inefficiencies of a particular construction, and opens a route to designing minimal abstractions through ideas from optimal coding.

G. Delimpaltadakis and G. Gleizer
An Information Theory of Finite Abstractions and their Fundamental Scalability Limits

02 / OPTIMIZATION & CONTROL

Safe feedback optimization

Steering a system towards an optimal operating point while respecting safety constraints along the way.

An optimal steady state can be safe even when the journey towards it is not. Feedback optimization uses measurements to continually adjust a system’s inputs, but enforcing constraints only at equilibrium can allow dangerous transient violations. With Pol Mestres, Jorge Cortés and Maurice Heemels, I develop a controller that combines safe gradient flows with high-order control barrier functions to enforce both state and input constraints throughout operation.

The controller solves a quadratic program online. Under the paper’s assumptions and from admissible initial conditions, we establish safety guarantees and conditions linking closed-loop equilibria to the optimization problem’s critical points. We also prove local convergence to interior optima, and global convergence in certain convex settings under additional conditions.

Why it matters

For physical systems, reaching a good operating point is only part of the task: the path must also be safe. This work brings transient safety into feedback optimization while explicitly addressing its interaction with stability and optimality.

G. Delimpaltadakis*, P. Mestres*, J. Cortés and W. P. M. H. Heemels
Safe Feedback Optimization through Control Barrier Functions
